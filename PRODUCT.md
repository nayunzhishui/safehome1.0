# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary users are parents who are trying to respond more steadily during everyday parent-child conflict, such as homework, bedtime, phone use, and communication breakdowns. They may be tired, worried, or unsure what to say next, so the interface must feel calm, concrete, and low-pressure.

Secondary users include supervisors, researchers, and project maintainers who review records, content, reports, and risk cues in the web backend. Student-facing assessment and profile features exist as a supporting evaluation engine, but the parent companion loop remains the main product surface.

## Product Purpose

SafeHome / ReadFeedback helps parents record specific emotional events, notice interaction patterns, receive non-diagnostic supportive feedback, practice small UP-informed training cards, check in on attempts, review weekly progress, and request human supervision when needed.

Success means a user can quickly understand what to do next, record one real event without feeling judged, and leave with one practical next step. The product must not present itself as clinical diagnosis, treatment, crisis intervention, or legal judgment.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, and humane. The product voice should sound like a careful companion: specific, plain-spoken, supportive, and restrained. It should avoid hype, performance pressure, medical authority, and generic inspirational language.

## Anti-references

This should not look or sound like a hospital system, a diagnostic testing portal, a marketing landing page, a children's game, or an AI-generated wellness template. Avoid over-decorated cards, large gradients, decorative light blobs, excessive leaves or icons, therapy-sounding claims, and buttons like "立即提升" or "一键生成".

## Design Principles

1. Start from the user's next small action, not from feature promotion.
2. Keep the core loop visible: record, understand, practice, review, and seek support.
3. Use concrete everyday language instead of abstract growth slogans.
4. Make safety and non-diagnostic boundaries clear without creating alarm.
5. Preserve consistency across miniprogram, web backend, content, and shared data contracts.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA where the platform allows it. Body text and muted text must remain readable on warm and tinted surfaces. Motion should be minimal and state-based, with reduced-motion-safe alternatives. The UI should support tired or stressed users by keeping one primary action per screen, generous touch targets, clear empty states, and non-judgmental error messages.
